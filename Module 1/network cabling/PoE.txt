Power over Ethernet (PoE)
- Definition: Sends both data and electrical power over a single Ethernet cable.
- Common cable: Cat5e or better (Cat6/6a for higher power).
- Standard: IEEE 802.3af, 802.3at, 802.3bt.
- PSE (Power Sourcing Equipment): Supplies power (PoE switch or injector).
- PD (Powered Device): Receives power (camera, phone, AP, etc.).
- Voltage: About 44-57 V DC.
- Max distance: 100 m (328 ft) per Ethernet standard.
- Pairs used: 2 pairs for lower power, 4 pairs for higher power.

Power levels:
- PoE (802.3af): up to 15.4 W at PSE, about 12.95 W at PD.
- PoE+ (802.3at): up to 30 W at PSE, about 25.5 W at PD.
- PoE++ / 4PPoE (802.3bt Type 3): up to 60 W at PSE, about 51 W at PD.
- PoE++ (802.3bt Type 4): up to 90 W at PSE, about 71.3 W at PD.

How it works:
- PSE detects a valid PD before sending full power.
- PSE classifies the PD to know how much power to allocate.
- Power and data travel together on the same cable.
- Power is negotiated and monitored.

Benefits:
- One cable for data + power.
- No separate AC outlet needed.
- Easy installation and relocation.
- Central power management and remote reboot.
- Safer than running separate power lines in many cases.

Limitations:
- 100 m distance limit.
- Limited power budget per switch/port.
- Heat and cable quality matter at higher power.
- PoE switches/injectors cost more than normal switches.
- Not all devices support PoE.

Common uses:
- IP cameras
- VoIP phones
- Wireless access points (APs)
- Access control systems
- IoT sensors
- LED lighting
- Digital signage

Common devices:
- PoE switch
- Midspan injector
- PoE splitter
- PoE extender

Simple rule:
Need data + power over one Ethernet cable within 100 m = PoE.
Check the PoE standard and power budget before connecting the device.
